Hankel strategy accuracy as a function of the GDAB Hurst exponent H
===================================================================

Measured 3 September 2026 against compute_analytical_gdab, the closed form, so
these are true errors and not run-to-run differences.

Sections 1 to 5 and the raw data at the bottom are that sweep. Section 6 was
added on 7 September 2026 and continues it below H = -1/2, against a different
reference; read its setup separately.

Setup
-----
  A = 10, eta = 1e-4, nu = 0, H swept from 2.0 down to -0.49 (its lower bound).
  z = 5 .. 100 in 12 points, chosen so that eta^2 G(z) stays well above
  underflow and a relative error on it still means something.
  strategy_params: n_eval = 250, eps_rel = 1e-12, f_max = 1e-4.
  gcc 11.4, system Boost, one machine.

Why H matters: the integrand is q*I(q) ~ q^-(2+2H). At H = 2 it dies as q^-6;
at H = -0.49 as q^-1.02, which is only barely convergent. Almost all of the
integral then sits in a very long tail, and G(z) in the far field falls seven
or more orders of magnitude below G(0), so a strategy has to resolve a small
number against a large total.

Two metrics are reported because they answer different questions and they do
NOT agree in direction:

  (T)   relative error of the transform itself, |T(z)/2pi - eta^2 G(z)|
        / |eta^2 G(z)|. This is the number to care about if you want the
        correlation function G(z), including its tail.

  (O)   relative error of the observable, eta^2 [G(z) - G(0)]. This is what a
        SESANS measurement is sensitive to.

Metric (O) improves for every strategy as H falls, but that is not the
quadrature getting better: G(0) = V^2 / [2 pi A^2 (1 + 2H)] diverges as
H -> -1/2, so the quantity in the denominator grows faster than the error
does. Metric (T) is the one that reflects the numerical difficulty.

2. STRATEGIES THAT HOLD UP  (metric T, essentially flat in H)
--------------------------------------------------------------
  These are insensitive to H across the whole range. They are not the most
  accurate, but their error does not move:

   1.  DHT_Guptasarma       3.2e-08 -> 1.1e-08   (improves slightly)
   2.  DHT_Key_101          8.0e-08 -> 1.7e-08   (improves slightly)
   3.  DHT_Anderson_801     1.5e-06 -> 1.0e-06   (improves slightly)
   4.  DHT_Guptasarma_Fast  3.1e-06 -> 4.6e-06   (flat within a factor 1.5)

  All four are digital filters with enough taps to span the slow tail, which
  is presumably why the tail decay rate does not reach them. DHT_Anderson_801
  is the clearest case: 801 taps, and the error actually falls as H does.


3. STRATEGIES THAT DEGRADE MOST  (metric T, ratio H = 2.0 -> H = -0.49)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
   1.  Fixed_DE_Ogata       2.8e-02 -> 2.2e+02      7800x
   2.  Adaptive_DE_Ooura    6.0e-14 -> 4.5e-11       750x
   3.  DHT_Key_201          6.3e-14 -> 4.0e-11       630x
   4.  QWE_Chave            1.2e-12 -> 4.2e-11        35x
   5.  DHT_Key_51           7.1e-06 -> 1.4e-04        19x
   6.  QWE_Key              4.1e-12 -> 4.2e-11        10x

  Read this alongside list 1. Apart from Ogata, the strategies that degrade
  most are the ones that were near machine precision to begin with, and they
  are STILL the most accurate at H = -0.49. Losing three orders of magnitude
  from 6e-14 leaves you at 4.5e-11; holding steady at 1.5e-06 does not. A
  large degradation ratio is not by itself a reason to avoid a strategy.

  DHT_Key_51 is the one case where the ratio and the absolute number agree:
  worst but one at every H, and getting worse.


4. Fixed_DE_Ogata
------------------
  Separate because it is not merely inaccurate. On metric T it is wrong by
  280% at H = 2 and by a factor of 220 at H = -0.49, and it returns success
  every time. It was run here with f_max = 1e-4; at the default f_max = 1.0 it
  is worse still. It only looks tolerable on metric O (max 1.3e-03) because
  its error is roughly a constant offset, which is small next to G(0) but
  enormous next to G(z) in the tail.

  Do not use it for GDAB at any H without first tuning f_max to the integrand,
  and do not trust its return status as evidence that the answer is good.
  (This is the same defect recorded in examples/sesans/.)


5. CAVEAT: the 4e-11 floor
---------------------------
  Four independent methods - a 201-tap filter, Ooura's double exponential, and
  both QWE variants - all land within 10% of 4.2e-11 at H = -0.49. Four
  unrelated algorithms do not usually agree to that precision on their own
  error. The floor is far more likely to come from something they share: the
  evaluation of form_factor_g_dab, or of the reference itself, where
  sf_poch(H, 1.5) = tgamma(H + 1.5) / tgamma(H) is being formed close to the
  pole of tgamma at H = 0 and 1 + 2H = 0.02 amplifies whatever is left.

  So treat 4e-11 at H = -0.49 as the resolution of this experiment, not as a
  measured property of those four strategies. Any comparison between them
  below that level is not supported by this data.

  RESOLVED, 7 September 2026: it was the reference. Re-running this exact grid
  at H = -0.49 against the 50-digit mpmath closed form of section 6, changing
  nothing else, gives

      DHT_Key_201        5.8e-13     (4.0e-11 above)
      Adaptive_DE_Ooura  4.4e-12     (4.5e-11)
      QWE_Key            9.6e-12     (4.2e-11)
      QWE_Chave          9.7e-12     (4.2e-11)
      DHT_Guptasarma     1.1e-08     (1.1e-08, unchanged)

  The four were never tied, and they spread over a factor 17 once the
  reference is taken out. compute_analytical_gdab is itself only good to about
  4e-11 at H = -0.49, for the sf_poch / (1 + 2H) reason guessed at above; it
  is fine for everything coarser, which is every other row of the raw data.
  The (T) figures in section 1 for those four strategies are therefore upper
  bounds, not measurements. Section 6 does not have this problem.


6. BELOW THE BOUND: H < -1/2
-----------------------------
  Measured 7 September 2026. Everything above stops at H = -0.49 because
  compute_analytical_gdab rejects H <= -1/2 and there is then nothing to
  compare against. But that bound belongs to the observable, not to the
  transform, and the two part company exactly here.

  Write s for the exponent of the integrand, q*I(q) ~ q^-s, s = 2 + 2H:

    s > 1      (H > -1/2)   int q I(q) dq converges absolutely. This is G(0).

    s > -1/2   (H > -5/4)   G(0) is infinite, but T(z) at z > 0 still
                            converges, conditionally: J0 supplies q^-1/2 of
                            decay and, more to the point, sign changes.

    s <= -1/2  (H <= -5/4)  q*I(q) grows and the integral diverges in the
                            classical sense. Its Abel sum is still the
                            analytic continuation, and every method that
                            accelerates an alternating series finds it -
                            mpmath's quadosc, summing over the zeros of J0,
                            agrees with the closed form to 40 digits at
                            H = -1.3, z = 20.

  So crossing s = 1 costs G(0), not T(z). Metric (O) above, eta^2[G(z)-G(0)],
  is -infinity everywhere for H <= -1/2: the model is dead for SESANS there,
  the transform is not. Only metric (T) is reported below.

  Setup
  -----
  Reference is the closed form of the transform itself, at 50 digits in
  mpmath 1.4.1:

      T(z) = (C / A^2) (z / 2A)^nu K_nu(z/A) / Gamma(p)
      p = H + 3/2,  nu = p - 1,  C = [(2A)^3 (H)_{3/2} eta]^2 pi^3

  This is the analytic continuation, so it stays finite for z > 0 past every
  threshold above. Form (H)_{3/2} as gamma(H + 3/2) * rgamma(H), not as a
  ratio of gammas, or it raises at H = -1 and -2.

  Driven through the Python extension, libhankel.hankel_transform(0, "gdab",
  z, [A, H, eta], name, params), A = 10, eta = 1e-4, same strategy_params as
  above (n_eval 250, eps_rel 1e-12, f_max 1e-4). z = 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 and
  the worst relative error over the five is reported. Note this is a coarser
  z grid than sections 1 to 5, so columns are comparable in magnitude but not
  point for point.

  What to read off it
  -------------------
  Nothing happens at H = -1/2. Several strategies are slightly better at
  H = -0.6 than at -0.49; the loss of absolute convergence is invisible to
  all ten. Behaviour splits three ways as s goes negative:

  * QWE_Key and QWE_Chave stay flat at ~1e-11 to the end, and overtake
    DHT_Key_201 at H ~ -0.95. Expected: they integrate between successive
    zeros of J0 and Shanks-extrapolate the alternating series, which is a
    summation method for exactly this. Adaptive_DE_Ooura, whose nodes
    converge onto the same zeros, loses three decades but stays usable
    (2.3e-08 at H = -1.49). See the crossover sweep below.

  * The digital filters degrade, and the widest one detonates.
    DHT_Anderson_801 is wrong by a factor 550 at H = -1.2 and by 3e+16 at
    -1.49, returning success every time. 801 taps span the most decades in q,
    so once q*I(q) grows it is the far end of the filter that dominates the
    sum, and truncation that never mattered becomes the entire answer. The
    short filters fail gracefully by comparison; DHT_Key_201 is still at
    2e-06 at H = -1.4.

  * Fixed_DE_Ogata was already unusable at H = -0.49 and stays that way.

  The crossover is at H ~ -0.95, but read it as soft. QWE is not improving:
  it sits in a flat 1e-12 to 1e-11 band all the way from H = +2 to -1.49.
  DHT_Key_201 is the one that moves, four decades in 0.4 of H. The crossover
  is only where the rising curve meets the flat band, and at -0.95 the gap
  (1.7e-12 vs 1.1e-12) is smaller than QWE's own scatter between adjacent H.
  Between -0.9 and -1.0 the two are tied and this data cannot separate them.
  The separation is decisive from -1.05 down: 30x there, 150x at -1.1.

  Adaptive_DE_Ooura crosses DHT_Key_201 at about the same place but stays
  5 to 10x behind QWE below -1.05.

  Recommendation below H = -1: QWE_Key or QWE_Chave. Do not use
  DHT_Anderson_801 or Fixed_DE_Ogata at any H < -1 - both are silently wrong
  by orders of magnitude.

  Two holes at exact integers
  ---------------------------
  sf_poch(H, 1.5) = tgamma(H + 1.5) / tgamma(H) sits on the poles of tgamma:

    H = -1.0, H = -2.0   T(z) is nan, silently, for every strategy; QWE also
                         reports "did not converge". The limit is finite (the
                         pole is in the denominator), so this is the ratio
                         being formed rather than the mathematics.
    H = 0.0              T(z) is exactly 0.0, since 1/gamma(0) = 0. That is
                         what (0)_{3/2} is, so it may be right for this
                         normalisation - but H = 0 is an ordinary Hurst value
                         and an identically zero form factor there is worth a
                         second look at the prefactor.

  Neither is caused by low H; they would be there at any H hitting a pole.

Scope
-----
One form factor (GDAB), one A, one nu, one z range, one machine. GDAB is
smooth and monotonically decaying, the friendliest case for a digital filter -
which is why the filters do so well here. An oscillatory form factor would
very likely reorder these lists. The tolerances in
tests/test_strategies_vs_analytical.c were set from the (O) column.
